# Did undercurrent predict dair.ai's Papers of the Week?

_Generated by `tools/dair_compare.py` on 2026-11-09. Database snapshot: map run 20261107T064547Z._

## Short answer

Not in the sense of "undercurrent picks the papers dair later features". What the data does show: dair picks sit in topics and use phrases that undercurrent had already flagged as rising, far more often than an average CS paper, and still more often than other LLM/agent papers from the same month, but the second edge is modest.

The big topic-level number is close to a two-topic story. 43.0% of picks were in a topic growing at least x1.41 when their listing month began, vs 4.9% of all CS papers from the same month. But 95.3% of those hits come from two topics, "LLM Agent Tools and Safety" and "LLM Mathematical Reasoning", the two large LLM waves of 2025 and 2026. Take those two topics out and picks were in a growing topic 4.3% of the time, against a base rate of 11.2% for LLM/agent papers of the same month. On a typical cutoff only 3 of 114 topics are growing at x1.41, so "in a growing topic" mostly means "in one of the two LLM topics".

The two tests with no hindsight in the grouping give the fairer size of the edge over other LLM/agent papers: neighbourhood growth lift 1.96 (CI 1.85 to 2.07) and rising-phrase lift 1.65 (CI 1.48 to 1.83). Both are well above 1, so dair's picks lean towards the faster-moving parts of LLM research more than the average LLM paper does. That is agreement between two trend-followers, not prediction of individual papers.

## What a fair website claim would be

The phrase test is the one to quote, because the phrase lists were frozen at each cutoff and the picks came later. Suggested wording:

> In a back-check on dair.ai's AI Papers of the Week (picks listed Jan 2025 to Mar 2026), 36.1% of featured papers used one of the 50 phrases undercurrent had flagged as rising before the paper appeared. For all CS papers from the same months the figure was 9.5%; for LLM and agent papers, 21.9%.

Keep the LLM comparison in the sentence. Without it the 3.8x figure overstates the case, because dair mostly features LLM papers and our rising phrases are mostly LLM phrases.

Do not claim:

- "undercurrent predicted dair's picks" or that it finds important papers. It ranks topics and phrases; inside a growing topic it says nothing about which paper will be featured.
- The topic-level lift (8.7x) as a headline. It rests on a clustering made with hindsight, and 95.3% of it is two LLM topics.
- Lead time as forecasting skill. The median unbroken growth streak before a listing (8 months) says the topic was already hot, which anyone reading arXiv would also see.

## Method

**dair list.** The yearly archive files (years/2023.md to years/2026.md in dair-ai/ML-Papers-of-the-Week) were parsed into (week, title, arXiv id). The arXiv id is taken from the row's "Paper" link, else the first arxiv.org link in the row. The listing date is the last day of the week. A paper listed twice counts once, at its first listing (4 repeats dropped).

**Topic growth.** Level-1 topics (the map's default level, 114 topics). For a pick listed in month W, the cutoff is the first day of W. Growth = ((papers in the topic in the 6 complete months before the cutoff + 1) / (papers in the 6 months before that + 1)) / (all mapped papers, same ratio), the same rule as `radar/serve.py` (a topic with 30+ recent and under 5 prior papers counts as new, so as growing; under 10 recent papers counts as not growing). Only papers submitted before the cutoff enter the count. Growing = x1.41 or more, growing fast = x2 or more.

**Base rates.** For each pick, the share of papers submitted in the same month as the pick whose topic was growing at the same cutoff, among (a) all mapped papers, (b) papers with the same primary arXiv category, (c) papers whose title or abstract matches `language model`, `LLM(s)` or `agent`, (d) papers in the same coarse map area (level 2, 21 areas) as the pick. The base rate reported is the average over picks of these matched shares, so it is weighted the same way as the hit rate. Lift = hit rate / matched base rate. 95% CIs come from 4,000 bootstrap resamples of picks; z is a one-sided Poisson-binomial test of hits against matched base rates.

**Neighbour check.** For each pick, its 100 nearest papers (cosine on SPECTER2 vectors) among papers submitted in the 12 months before the cutoff; growth = (recent 6 months + 1) / (prior 6 + 1) / CS volume ratio. Base: up to 150 random papers from the pick's submission month (and 150 LLM/agent papers), same cutoff. SPECTER2 is a fixed pretrained model and the neighbours are only earlier papers, so nothing here was fit on data after the cutoff.

**Phrase test.** Cutoffs with a stored S3 run: 2025-01-01, 2025-04-01, 2025-07-01, 2025-10-01. The top 50 phrases by score at each cutoff (latest run per tag, as in `radar/backtest.py`) are matched in title + abstract with the same tokenizer (stop words dropped, so "chain of thought" is stored as "chain thought"). Picks count if listed in the 6 months after the cutoff and submitted on or after it (so they could not have shaped the phrase list). Base: share of all papers submitted in the pick's month containing one of the 50 phrases, and the same among LLM/agent papers. Cutoffs are 3 months apart with 6-month windows, so a pick can appear under two cutoffs; the CI resamples picks, not pairs.

## Bias and caveats (read before quoting any number)

1. **The topics know the future.** Topic assignments come from one clustering of all papers through Nov 2026. A cluster that exists in 2025 may exist only because of papers that came later, so "the topic was growing before the pick" partly uses hindsight about which papers belong together. Counting only papers before the cutoff removes lookahead in the counts, not in the grouping. The neighbour check and the phrase test have no such lookahead; weigh them more.
2. **The LLM wave confounds everything.** dair is an AI-paper newsletter; 89.9% of the scored picks are LLM/agent papers. Our growing topics are mostly LLM/agent topics. Lift against all CS mostly measures "dair covers LLMs". The LLM/agent base rate is the fairer comparison, and the keyword filter used for it is crude (it misses some LLM papers and includes some RL "agent" papers).
3. **dair's sample is not random.** It is a curated newsletter; 353 of 1990 picks have no arXiv link (blog posts, reports) and drop out. Only 1194 of 1633 unique arXiv picks are on our map (corpus is cs.* only, from Nov 2023).
4. **Short history.** Growth needs 12 months, so the topic test starts in Nov 2024; lead times that reach that month are censored.
5. **Submission dates are month-level** in our papers table. 71.6% of scored picks were submitted in their listing month (after the cutoff), 26.7% the month before (so the pick itself is one paper inside its topic's recent window, a negligible effect), and 1.6% earlier.
6. **The phrase lists are generic in places** ("ai systems", "llm agents", "reasoning models"), which raises hit rates for any LLM paper. That is why the LLM/agent base rate matters.
