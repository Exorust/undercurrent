What is undercurrent?

It places every computer-science paper on arXiv on one map, so papers on similar problems sit near each other and form topics. Each topic shows how fast it is growing, corrected for the growth of CS as a whole, so a topic only looks hot if it is outpacing the field. Lines between topics are made of bridge papers: papers whose nearest neighbours sit in both topics.

It is for researchers looking for methods arriving from other areas, and for neighbouring fields worth reading. It is a monthly snapshot, not a live feed, built by Chandrahas Aroori.

Method

How the numbers are made

Every number on the map comes from one of these rules. They are simple on purpose, so you can check them.

Growth

Papers in the window against papers in the window before it, divided by the growth of all CS over the same months. 1.0 means a topic keeps pace with the field.

growth = ((papers now + 1) / (papers before + 1)) / CS growth
Arriving sub-topic

A sub-topic whose recent papers make up a clearly larger share of it than they do of the whole corpus.

≥ 30 papers in the window and window share ≥ 1.6 × corpus share
Bridge paper and link strength

A paper is a bridge when its nearest neighbours fall in two topics at once. A link's strength is its bridge count scaled by the size of both topics, so two big topics do not link just because they are big.

bridge: ≥ 5 of its 14 nearest neighbours in each topic
strength = bridges / √(n₁ × n₂)
Map and topics

Each paper's title and abstract become a SPECTER2 embedding from Semantic Scholar. A 15-neighbour graph is laid out with UMAP, and HDBSCAN groups the layout into topics and sub-topics.

SPECTER2 → 15-neighbour graph → UMAP → HDBSCAN
How much to trust it

I tested whether rising phrases keep rising. Going back to four earlier cutoffs, the top 50 rising phrases went on to grow 1.5× faster than CS in 36 % of cases, against a 27 % baseline. That is a real but modest lift: treat the map as a place to start reading, not a forecast.
